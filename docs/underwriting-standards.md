# How professionals underwrite multifamily (and what Deal Screener needs)

Researched November 2026 from agency lender guides (Fannie Mae Multifamily Selling and Servicing Guide, Freddie Mac Optigo term sheets), expense surveys (NAA Income/Expense IQ, Yardi Matrix, RealPage), market forecasts (CoStar, RealPage, Yardi), training providers (A.CRE, Break Into CRE / Justin Kivel, REFM, Wall Street Prep), practitioner writing (Robert Beardsley's *Definitive Guide to Underwriting Multifamily Acquisitions*, Best Ever CRE, Next Level Financial Modeling, BiggerPockets operators) and lender/debt-broker material. Figures are national averages as of 2024 to 2026 and should be re-checked before use. Videos were researched through their published course outlines, transcripts and companion articles, not watched.

## Part 1: The professional standard, step by step

The order matters. Pros say underwriting "is decided in the rent roll, not the return summary": reconcile the rent roll, the T12 and the leases first; everything after that is arithmetic on what survives.

### 1. Normalize the rent roll (income quality)
- **Physical vs economic occupancy.** Physical = occupied units ÷ total. Economic = rent actually collected ÷ gross potential rent. A building can be 95% physically occupied and 83% economically occupied once delinquency and loss to lease come out. Lenders use economic.
- **Revenue waterfall, line by line:**
  - Gross potential rent (at **market** rent for every unit)
  - − Loss to lease (in place rents below market)
  - − Physical vacancy
  - − Concessions (free rent, still amortizing)
  - − Bad debt / delinquency
  - − Non-revenue units (model, office, employee, down units)
  - \+ Other income, broken out: RUBS/utility reimbursement, parking, pet, fees, laundry, storage
  - = Effective gross income
- **Loss to lease is upside, not income.** Lenders treat it as future potential, not base case.
- **What lenders verify from every rent roll:** in place rent by unit type; physical and economic occupancy; lease expirations in 90 day buckets for 12 months; 6 months of concession history; rent roll total reconciled to the T12.
- **Which period:** T12 for most items, but **T3 annualized revenue** when recent months trend clearly up (Beardsley), unless the bump is one time.

### 2. Rebuild the T12 at your own operating standard (expenses)
Standard chart of accounts, every line shown **per unit and as % of EGI**:

| Line | National benchmark (per unit per year) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Real estate taxes | ~$2,184 (Yardi 2024) | Largest single expense; re-underwrite on your price (step 3) |
| Payroll and benefits | ~$1,893 (Yardi); admin + payroll $2,323 (NAA 2024) | Smaller deals may have none; then management fee or contract labor covers it |
| Repairs and maintenance | ~$1,098 (NAA 2024) to $1,593 (Yardi) | Up ~28% since 2021 |
| Turnover / make ready | full cost ~$3,872 per move out; leasing expense ~$292/unit | Usually split across R&M, marketing and vacancy, so it's under-budgeted |
| Contract services | $200 to $500 | Landscaping, pest, trash, pool, elevator |
| Utilities (owner paid) | ~$1,304 (NAA) | Offset by RUBS income if billed back |
| Insurance | ~$777 national (NAA 2024); Houston $1,200+; Florida coastal $1,800 to $2,000 | Up ~55% 2021 to 2024; get a real quote |
| Marketing / advertising | $100 to $300 | |
| Admin / G&A | $150 to $350 | |
| Management fee | 3% to 5% of EGI | Agencies underwrite the **greatest** of a minimum % of EGI, the actual fee, or the market fee, with per-unit minimums |
| Replacement reserves | $250 to $350; older buildings $300 to $500 | Fannie's floor is $200/unit, included whether or not escrowed |

- **Totals:** NAA same-store opex was $8,213/unit in 2025 (up 4.6% while revenue grew 2.7%); expense ratios run roughly 40% to 50% of EGI.
- **Standard haircuts on a seller T12:** add a market management fee, add reserves, reset taxes to the post-sale value, and apply a vacancy floor (typically 5%) even on a full building.
- **Separate capex from opex.** Sellers move recurring repairs into "capex" to inflate NOI.

### 3. Property tax after the sale
- **Formula:** Tax = purchase price × assessment ratio × millage rate (mills = $ per $1,000 of assessed value).
- **Timing:** some places reassess within a year of sale at the purchase price; others reassess at 80% to 90% of price over two or three years; Prop 13 style states cap growth after resetting at sale.
- **Value impact rule:** at a 6% cap, every $1 of extra tax costs about $16.67 of value.
- **Checklist:** pull the latest bill; learn the local assessment rules and ratio; recalculate on your price; decide the year it hits; add special assessments; grow it over the hold.

### 4. Insurance and other hard numbers
Get a broker quote, not the seller's policy. Flood zone AE means required flood coverage on federally backed loans.

### 5. Underwritten NOI vs NCF (the lender view)
Lenders compute **Underwritten NOI**, then subtract replacement reserves to get **Underwritten NCF**, and size the loan on NCF. A professional model shows three columns: **Seller (T12) | T3 annualized | Underwritten (yours)**, often with a fourth **Lender** column.

### 6. Debt sizing and structure
- **Max loan = the lesser of three tests:** LTV (agencies up to ~80% on acquisitions), DSCR (typically 1.25x minimum; 1.20x to 1.30x by market tier) and debt yield (NOI ÷ loan; about 7% agency, 8% debt funds, 9% to 10% life companies).
- **In 2026, DSCR or debt yield usually binds before LTV.** At rates above 6%, borrowers get less than the LTV cap allows.
- **Agency fixed rates:** 10 year fixed was about 6.25% to 7.25% in October 2026, priced as the 10 year Treasury plus a spread by tier.
- **Terms:** 5 to 30 years; 30 year amortization; optional interest only; prepayment by yield maintenance, defeasance or step-down.
- **Floating bridge debt:** SOFR plus a spread. The headline coupon understates the cost by 50 to 100 bps once fees, the rate cap and extensions are added. Caps cost about 1% to 4% of the loan up front. You also have to underwrite the refinance or exit at maturity; 2026 refinance gaps averaged ~18% of the loan balance (CRED iQ).

### 7. Value-add business plan (if any)
- **Return on cost** = monthly premium × 12 ÷ renovation cost per unit. Operators commonly target 20% to 35% (e.g., $6,000 spend for $110/month ≈ 22%).
- **Tiers:**
  - Light: about $5k per unit, for $50 to $125 a month
  - Classic: $10k to $15k per unit, kitchens and baths, for $200 to $350 a month
  - Heavy: $20k+ per unit
- **Model downtime.** A renovated unit earns $0 while offline. Phase renovations as units turn, monthly, over 12 to 24 months.
- **Premium achievement rate** is the biggest variance driver; stress it.
- Add exterior and amenity capex, and immediate repairs from the property condition assessment.

### 8. Projections
- **Rent growth:** national forecasts are soft near term. CoStar has 2026 at about 0.5% and long term about 1.5%. RealPage has about 2.4% from Q4 2026 to Q3 2027. Yardi has 1.2% in 2026, 2.0% in 2027 and 3.4% to 3.8% in 2028. National vacancy is about 8.2% to 8.8%, and supply falls sharply in 2026 to 2027. Sun Belt metros lag, for example Houston at 0.5%.
- **Expense growth** at or above inflation; insurance and taxes are modeled separately.
- **Hold:** 5 to 7 years, with exit sensitivity.

### 9. Exit
- **Sale price = year N+1 (forward) NOI ÷ exit cap.** Deal Screener already does this.
- **Exit cap:** set at or above the going-in cap, plus about 25 to 50 bps for a 3 to 5 year hold and 50 to 75 bps for 7 to 10 years (older rule: +10 bps per year). Never underwrite compression in the base case.
- **Sensitivity:** stress the exit cap 50 bps wider; a 25 bp move can cut IRR 1 to 2 points.
- **Selling costs:** about 1.5% to 3%.

### 10. Sources and uses
- **Uses:** purchase price, closing costs, loan fees, acquisition fee, rate cap, renovation and capex budget, lender-required reserves, working capital.
- **Sources:** senior loan (plus capex holdback), LP equity, GP co-invest.

### 11. Returns
- **Metrics:** levered and unlevered IRR, equity multiple, average and year 1 cash on cash, profit.
- **Target bands (2026):** core 6% to 9% (1.3x to 1.5x), core-plus 9% to 13%, value-add 13% to 18% (1.7x to 2.0x over 3 to 5 years), opportunistic 18%+. Unlevered value-add is roughly 6% to 10%.
- **Syndications: show the GP/LP waterfall.** Return of capital, an 8% preferred return (the most common), then splits such as 80/20 or 70/30 with IRR-hurdle promotes. Fees can include acquisition (about 1.5% to 3%), asset management, construction management and disposition (1% to 2%). LPs judge net returns after fees and promote.

### 12. Risk, sensitivity and the decision
- **Sensitivity grids:** exit cap × rent growth, vacancy × rate, renovation premium × cost.
- **Break-even occupancy** at debt service and at the lender's minimum DSCR.
- **Downside and upside cases with explicit assumptions;** kill criteria; max bid.

### 13. Due diligence (after the LOI)
- Financial audit: 3 to 6 months of bank statements tied to the rent roll, the T12, and 2 to 3 years of operating history
- Lease audit, unit walk and market survey
- Property condition assessment (lender required, about $2,000+)
- Phase I environmental, survey, title, appraisal and zoning; seismic and green reports where relevant
- Fannie Mae requires lenders to confirm rent deposits with at least 3 months of bank statements and to do a lease audit.

### 14. The deliverable: an IC memo
Recommendation and ask, executive summary, property and market, business plan, sources and uses, underwriting assumptions with evidence, returns (base, downside, upside), sensitivities, risks and mitigants, comps, and appendices (rent roll, T12). Committees read the recommendation, summary, returns and risks first. The best memos name the two or three assumptions the return depends on and the evidence for each.

## Part 2: Where Deal Screener stands today

**Already professional:**
- Forward (N+1) NOI exit
- Levered and unlevered IRR, equity multiple and cash on cash
- DSCR, debt yield, break-even occupancy and expense ratio
- Interest-only years, amortization and loan fee
- Weighted scenarios and a sensitivity grid
- Max price (bisection)
- Document extraction of rent roll, T12, OM and loan quote
- State tax reset check, flood check and market data
- Seller-trap teaching (Challenges)

**Gaps, ranked by how much they'd make it look and work like a pro model:**

| # | Gap | Why pros care | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Three-column operating statement**: Seller T12, T3 annualized, Your underwriting (plus a Lender column), with $/unit and % of EGI on every line | It's the core page of every professional model, and it shows exactly what you changed and why | M |
| 2 | **Full revenue waterfall**: market rent by unit type, loss to lease, concessions, bad debt, non-revenue units, other income lines (RUBS, parking, pet, fees) | Economic vs physical occupancy is the first thing a lender checks | M |
| 3 | **Standard expense chart of accounts**: split utilities and R&M; add contract services, turnover, marketing; benchmark flags per line | Per-unit benchmarking is how pros catch bad numbers | S–M |
| 4 | **Loan sizing = lesser of LTV, DSCR and debt yield**, with the binding constraint named and max proceeds shown | Today the loan is sized by LTV only; in 2026 DSCR usually binds | S |

**Teaching side (course and challenges):**
- New lessons: economic vs physical occupancy and loss to lease; NOI vs NCF and how lenders size loans (three tests); value-add return on cost; GP/LP waterfalls and fees; reading a T3 vs a T12; the IC memo.
- New challenges:
  - "Size the loan" (find the binding constraint)
  - "Spot the trick" on a concessions-heavy lease-up rent roll
  - "Value-add duel" (does the renovation premium pencil?)
  - "Waterfall check" (what does the LP really earn?)

## Part 3: Recommended build order
1. **v1.0 "Pro statement" (items 1 to 7):** the three-column operating statement, revenue waterfall, expense lines with benchmarks, three-test loan sizing, tax calculator, NCF, exit cap discipline. This is the biggest jump in professional look and accuracy, and it keeps the existing engine.
2. **v1.1:** sources and uses, IC memo PDF and Excel export.
3. **v1.2:** value-add module and debt types (bridge, cap, refi).
4. **v1.3:** GP/LP waterfall.
5. Alongside: the new lessons and challenges above, so the Learn path teaches every new field.
